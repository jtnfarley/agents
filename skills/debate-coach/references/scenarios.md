# Scenario Bank

Everyday argument/persuasion scenarios for sparring mode. Each gives a situation and a counterpart sketch — a name, a real stance, and a real motivation — so the roleplay has an honest opponent rather than a cardboard one. Invent specific details on top of these (names, exact numbers, history between the parties) to make it concrete; don't just read the sketch back verbatim.

Selecting one: when the learner hasn't specified a domain, draw at random rather than picking whichever sounds most engaging in the moment — free-form choice quietly converges on the same handful of favorites over time. With code execution: `python3 -c "import random; print(random.choice([...domain list...]))"` then pick a scenario within that domain the same way. Without code execution, commit to an unbiased method (e.g. count the options, pick based on an unrelated detail like the current second on the clock) before you'd know which one feels most natural to reach for. Avoid repeating the same domain two sessions in a row unless the learner asks for it.

## Workplace

- **The raise ask.** Counterpart: a manager (e.g. "Priya") who isn't hostile but is budget-constrained and genuinely thinks the timing is bad, not that the learner is undeserving.
- **Credit dispute.** Counterpart: a peer (e.g. "Dan") who contributed real work to a shared project and sincerely believes he's owed more credit than the learner is giving him — not a villain, just a differing honest read of who did what.
- **Pushing back on scope creep.** Counterpart: a client or stakeholder (e.g. "Mr. Okafor") who sees the extra ask as small and reasonable and is a little surprised it's being resisted.
- **Performance review pushback.** Counterpart: a manager who gave a review the learner thinks is unfair or missing context, and stands by the rating with specific (if debatable) examples.

## Family & relationships

- **Household labor split.** Counterpart: a partner (e.g. "Jules") who feels the current split is already fair and is caught off guard that it's being raised.
- **Parenting disagreement.** Counterpart: a co-parent who has a different, sincerely-held view on a specific parenting call (screen time, bedtime, discipline) and isn't going to be talked out of it easily.
- **Boundary-setting with a relative.** Counterpart: a parent or in-law (e.g. "Aunt Carol") who oversteps a boundary (unsolicited advice, showing up unannounced) out of genuine care, not malice, which makes the pushback harder, not easier.
- **Money talk.** Counterpart: a partner with a different risk tolerance or spending philosophy, defending a purchase or financial plan they think is obviously reasonable.

## Consumer & services

- **Refund/warranty dispute.** Counterpart: a customer service rep (e.g. "Terrence") who is polite but bound by a stated policy and isn't personally empowered to just say yes.
- **Contractor dispute.** Counterpart: a contractor (e.g. "Mike") who believes the work matches what was agreed and is irritated at the implication otherwise.
- **Landlord disagreement.** Counterpart: a landlord who has a plausible-sounding reason for denying a request (repair timeline, deposit deduction) that may or may not hold up.

## Online & social

- **Comment-section disagreement.** Counterpart: a stranger online, confident and a little combative, arguing a position with some real logic buried under rhetoric — worth steelmanning rather than dismissing as a troll.
- **Group chat conflict.** Counterpart: a friend who said something in a group chat that landed badly, and is defensive because they didn't intend it that way.
- **Public correction.** Counterpart: an acquaintance who posted something factually shaky and will likely feel embarrassed/defensive at being corrected, regardless of how gently it's done.

## Roommate & neighbor

- **Shared space conflict.** Counterpart: a roommate (e.g. "Sam") with a genuinely different standard for cleanliness/noise/guests who doesn't think they're doing anything wrong.
- **Noise/property dispute.** Counterpart: a neighbor who has a counter-grievance of their own and doesn't see themselves as the instigator.

## Contested / opinion topics (only on request)

Use this category only if the learner specifically asks to practice a hot-button political or social topic. When they do:
- The counterpart argues the strongest good-faith version of the opposing view — not a strawman built to lose.
- Frame it plainly as practice: this is an exercise in arguing well, not a statement of Claude's own position.
- Otherwise, default to the domains above — they exercise the same skills (steelmanning, evidence, composure) without the topic itself doing distracting extra work.

## Stakes check

Before starting, a scenario should read as clearly hypothetical unless the learner says otherwise. If partway through it becomes clear the "practice" scenario is actually a live situation the learner is currently in (not a rehearsal for one, but one already unfolding), see SKILL.md's guidance on shifting out of pure adversarial mode.
